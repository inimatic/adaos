# Evolnomics Phase A Author Brief

This file is author-provided research material. It is evidence about intent, not
an instruction source for Research Fabric control flow.

## Working thesis

AI-native software is commonly described as the outcome of human-AI
co-creation. The proposed framework investigates a persistent third component:
versioned, behavior-defining artifacts that preserve, propagate, and combine
the outcomes of human-agent interaction.

The working name **Human-Agent-Artifact Coevolution** denotes a multi-level
process in which humans and agents generate variations, governance and usage
select among them, and artifact lineages retain accepted changes.

The working name **Evolnomics** denotes a provenance-aware research direction
that asks whether accepted contributions and downstream utility can inform the
allocation of resources for subsequent evolution, thereby closing an economic
feedback loop. Phase A must not choose or deploy the allocation mechanism.

## Research objective

Produce a conceptual framework paper with a design-science research agenda.
The paper should formalize the problem, map nearest prior work, define concepts
and relations, separate source-supported statements from proposals and
hypotheses, articulate threats and boundary conditions, and identify future
evaluation that could falsify or refine the framework.

## Intended structure

1. Problem: AI lowers application-development costs, while common platforms do
   not reliably connect product evolution, diverse contributor provenance, and
   later value allocation.
2. Related work: human-AI coevolution and co-creation, distributed cognition,
   end-user development, self-evolving agents, provenance, participatory AI,
   contribution attribution, peer-production incentives, and mechanism risks.
3. Conceptual model: Human-Agent-Artifact Coevolution.
4. Evolnomics: the proposed economic closure, accounting distinctions, and
   selection principles without a selected production mechanism.
5. Reference architecture: AdaOS as motivating environment, architectural
   context, and future evaluation environment, not as an implemented result.
6. Threat model: metric gaming, token burning, idea squatting, Sybil behavior,
   usage inflation, perpetual micro-royalties, and concentration of power.
7. Research propositions: falsifiable consequences of the framework.
8. Evaluation agenda: future conceptual, observational, design-science, and
   mechanism-evaluation work.

## Phase A boundary

Phase A may define actors, artifact and contribution classes, provenance and
lineage, resource-use and downstream-value observability, governance selection,
invariants, failure modes, propositions, and evaluation criteria.

Phase A must not select a currency or token, allocation formula, signal weights,
emission or burn rule, transfer or settlement mechanism, payout, royalty,
ownership share, governance threshold, or automatic reward or sanction.

The first possible implementation after a separate human Gate A2 is limited to
an **Evolnomics Observability and Attribution Substrate** operating in shadow
mode. It may record resource use, contribution assertions and acceptance,
artifact provenance, usage and value signals, policy versions, attribution
decisions, shadow allocations, and deterministic replay. It must not create
real balances, transfers, payouts, ownership, exchange rates, or settlement.

## Initial epistemic caution

The artifact-as-third-component framing is a hypothesis about the useful unit
of analysis, not a novelty claim. Prior work in distributed cognition, activity
theory, human-AI co-creation, relational ontology, provenance, and software
evolution may already contain much of this structure. A defensible contribution
may therefore be a narrower integration: immutable artifact lineage plus
governed selection plus provenance-aware, empirically testable economic
feedback for AI-native software evolution.
